# A prospective year: recurring musical relationships

**Proposed, not validated or implemented.** Audience: self-directed pupils aged 11–13 with some instrumental experience. [Leadership decisions](../../CURRICULUM_DECISION_MEMO.md) and [research/access limits](evidence-brief.md) govern this proposal. Musical experiences and numerical planning choices here are our design assumptions, not an adopted national syllabus or demonstrated learning effect.

## Architecture and availability

Organise a prospective year around four families, revisited with different relations, contexts and pupil decisions. “Week” means a planning opportunity, not a deadline or prerequisite. Four nine-opportunity spirals plus two flexible opportunities give a 38-opportunity planning envelope; schools may shorten, reorder or repeat it. A missed encounter creates no catch-up debt. Do not add calendar locks, automatic level promotion or mandatory completion.

| Recurring family | Initial relation | Later revisit | Real-world connection | Current content boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pulse, groove and space | Distinguish steady pulse, attacks, durations and silence. | Keep a pulse while varying density; coordinate a complementary role. | Tap/play a short pattern with a partner; distinguish note length from attack count. | Theme 02 implements rhythm practice and saved rhythms. Expanded metre, ensemble and repertoire proposals are not thereby implemented. |
| Motifs and conversation | Recognise/reproduce an opening or echo. | Keep one feature and vary another; contrast; revise a reply. | Sing/play a call and respond, then exchange roles. | Theme 01 supplies related ending work. Echo exists only in draft PR #9; the broader Theme 03 design is proposed. |
| Tonal relationships and layers | Hear a named reference pitch and compare a melody against it. | Choose a bass/anchor role; hear interactions and contextual endings. | Sustain a reference, then play alternative endings or parts together. | Existing auxiliary harmony/bass tools are resources, not proof of a reviewed family or implemented Themes 04–05. New contextual tasks need their own design. |
| Expression, phrase and form | Notice recurrence, breathing space and audible expressive choices. | Shape contrasting sections, returns and alternative interpretations. | Interpret a short phrase two ways; adapt breath/bow/attack deliberately. | Proposed family; available composition/phrase tools do not establish a complete expression curriculum. No new theme is enabled here. |

All future examples need contextual and rights review. A C-major piano miniature is a useful bounded starting point, not culturally neutral coverage. A local/pupil-chosen listening or instrument example should inform each family’s contextual revisit. Do not add a decorative genre label to unchanged material or claim a single drone represents an entire musical tradition. Use licensed/original material with appropriate cultural expertise; restricted traditions are not a content-generation prompt. No streaming or external runtime service is needed.

## Four spirals, not four ability levels

| Planning envelope | What changes musically | Suggested use of the four families | Revisit/product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opportunities 1–9: notice and reproduce | Short familiar relationships; sound before optional symbols; prediction then comparison. | Two opportunities per family, one to hear/explore and one to retain/play/revise. Creation is present in both. | Ninth: choose a fragment to replay, perform or keep. No formal mastery score. |
| 10–18: retain and change | Preserve an anchor while altering a named feature; distinguish literal copying from a purposeful variation. | Reuse earlier motifs/grooves in another playable register or context. Keep controls and length stable while changing the relation. | Ninth: compare an earlier saved idea with a chosen revision; the pupil may prefer either. |
| 19–27: coordinate and contrast | Hear two roles, contrasting contours/densities, sections and contextual endings. | Revisit each family through a partner/voice/instrument option and a solo alternative. Introduce one new demand at a time. | Ninth: combine two familiar ideas or perform two contrasting versions. |
| 28–36: choose, justify and transfer | Choose a brief or intention, shape a small form, listen, revise and explain an effect. | Repertoire/instrument choice becomes more prominent; short-pattern support remains available. | Ninth: a private mini-set, live demonstration or locally saved piece. Sharing is optional, never an upload requirement. |
| 37–38: local flexibility | Revisit a misconception, local practice, missed encounter or pupil interest. | Not two new compulsory themes. Can occur anywhere in the year. | Learner-selected return and a next-step suggestion. |

Two-opportunity family illustration: first encounter hears two versions, imitates a fragment, changes one feature and creates a reply; second re-hears the relation in a new context, plays away from the screen and revises a chosen idea. Both have a useful stopping point. Do not delay all creation until the second encounter or last spiral.

## Competency map: what stronger participation could look like

These descriptors guide design and observation; they are neither guaranteed annual outcomes nor an automated proficiency scale.

| Strand | Notice/reproduce | Retain/change | Coordinate/contrast | Choose/justify/transfer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audiation/listening | Hum/tap a predicted short ending before audition. | Predict the effect of changing one feature. | Compare a predicted relation with what is heard. | Describe which heard cues guided a choice; predictions may differ musically. |
| Rhythm | Find pulse; reproduce a short attack/rest pattern. | Retain pulse while changing density or durations. | Coordinate repeated and contrasting roles; hear a rest’s place. | Choose and revise a rhythmic role for an intended effect. |
| Melody | Echo and trace contour in a comfortable range. | Retain an opening; change an ending. | Contrast direction/register while retaining an anchor. | Shape and revise a melodic phrase; demonstrate it away from the screen. |
| Harmony | Hear a named pitch/chord reference in context. | Compare alternatives against that reference. | Hear melody/bass or anchor interactions. | Choose between contextual effects without treating taste as a correct answer. |
| Phrase/form | Notice recurrence and breathing space. | Connect two related phrases. | Contrast sections and recognise a return. | Make, perform and revise a small form. |
| Expression | Hear an actual articulation, dynamic or duration contrast. | Change one expressive feature while holding notes stable. | Compare combined expressive decisions. | Interpret the same material two ways and discuss the effect. |
| Improvisation/composition | Explore and keep an idea by choice. | Make a variation and a revision. | Combine, contrast and revise familiar ideas. | Select, perform and explain a small piece or improvised response. |
| Instrument transfer | Sing/tap/play a comfortable fragment. | Reproduce the relation on the pupil’s instrument. | Adapt key/register/breath/bow or role with appropriate support. | Demonstrate and reflect without relying on visible tiles. |

For every spiral, review the coverage across all eight strands. A blank strand is a planning gap to fill through a meaningful experience, not a reason to invent a quiz or claim an existing auxiliary tool completes it. Reflection may be spoken privately, demonstrated or thought through; literacy is not the musical target.

## Meaningful differentiation within every activity

A calendar spiral and a selectable level are different axes. The same level can recur across the year. A pupil may select level 1 for listening and level 4 for creation. Schools choose available implemented levels; pupils choose among those. Suggestions are optional and never alter placement or erase work.

| Configuration | Musical relation | Example bounded demand | Keep stable / scaffold | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1: short, supported noticing | Identify/reproduce one familiar relation. | One bar, few pitches or a short rhythm; audible demonstration. | Unlimited replay, sounded options, optional labels; no notation prerequisite. | Hear/sing/tap the relation. A correct click alone is insufficient. |
| 2: keep and change | Retain an anchor and vary one feature. | Same bar length and interface; change an ending while keeping rhythm. | Do not simultaneously increase pitch pool, memory, vocabulary and controls. | Constrained reply plus a freely made alternative. |
| 3: contrast and coordination | Compare two relations or roles in context. | Same-length contour/density contrast, or an anchor plus a melody. | Isolate roles/chunks; explain reference sounds and unassessed scaffolds. | Explain or demonstrate what differs; do not grade preferred effect. |
| 4: phrase and agency | Select constraints/intention, revise and transfer. | A small form or an optional timing extension; longer memory is not compulsory. | Keep short-pattern options, replay and familiar controls; separate new timing from increased length. | A deliberate revision and away-from-screen attempt; no automatic creativity score. |

Every completed activity design must replace these broad descriptors with its own exact four-configuration matrix: number/duration of events, metre, tempo, pitch/rhythm material, retained/changed relation, UI/memory demands and accepted-answer logic. If four musically distinct configurations cannot be justified, revise the activity concept instead of manufacturing harder versions. This proposal does not change the registry’s existing levels.

## Practical balance and stopping points

The existing three entrances remain **listening**, **reconstruction/problem solving**, and **creation**. Discovery and imitation can occur within listening; manipulation spans construction and creation. They are cognitive operations, not four additional navigation modes.

An illustrative 18-minute encounter allows discovery 3 minutes, imitation 3, manipulation 4, creation 6, reflection/transfer 2. This is a pilot assumption, not a required ratio or research-derived dosage. Offer a 3–5-minute option: hear a relation, try it, make one chosen change, stop. Offer a longer route: compare, reproduce, vary, listen to the result, revise or keep it. Stop at any useful musical point; there is no streak, timer bonus or penalty for replay.

A pupil can enter creation immediately, hear a model if wanted and revisit a listening scaffold without losing the draft. Motor speed, notation knowledge and interface familiarity must not silently become difficulty. Do not remove access aids to make the music “harder”. A pupil unable to use an instrument can sing, tap or use the app where appropriate; a rhythm-only adaptation cannot substantiate a melody/harmony outcome.

## Check the framework before scaling

Review each spiral for meaningful recurrence, new contexts, open creation, independent entry, accessible operation and feasible instrumental transfer. Pilot a small vertical slice, not a calendar of unfinished activities. Use [the common quality criteria](quality-review.md); no calendar slot becomes available school content until its actual implementation and evidence are eligible. Pupil data remains local, with no accounts, analytics or compulsory recordings.
